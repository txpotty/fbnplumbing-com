    Copper Service Line Installation in Charlottesville, VA

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. It has a long track record. We flare or use brass compression fittings at the ends, keep the run in one piece between them, and pad it with sand to keep stones off. Where it goes through a concrete wall, it gets a plastic sleeve and a seal to keep water and bugs out.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Charlottesville, VA

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Disconnecting hoses, shutting off outdoor spigots and keeping crawlspace vents closed in winter are simple ways to prevent freeze damage. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and keep it covered from sunlight at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Charlottesville

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. The zinc coating wears off and the steel rusts from the inside. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, which is why pressure drops and the water runs orange after a shutoff, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Emergency Water Line Replacement in Charlottesville

    Water bubbling up through the lawn or the street side of the sidewalk means a break, not a drip. First step, shut off the main valve. If that does not stop it, the break is on your side of the meter but before the valve, and we will shut it at the curb stop with a meter key and plan the replacement from there, usually starting the same or next day.

    PEX Service Lines for Long Runs in Charlottesville

    We buy PEX in long coils. That makes it the easy pick when the meter sits out at the road and the house is set far back, since a single coil reaches all the way without a splice. Fewer joints underground means fewer places a leak can start, and it is the joints that usually fail first.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Charlottesville

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years will wear through the pipe. It costs more than PEX.

    Trenchless Bores Under Driveways in Charlottesville

    Directional boring drills a path under the obstacle and pulls the pipe back through it. It does not need the old pipe as a guide. We use it when the new line has to take a different route, say around a new addition or under a driveway poured after the house was built, and the launch and exit pits stay small. Heavy rock is the exception.
