    Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in Charlottesville, VA

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. Plenty of leaks come from the drain valve, the relief valve or a sweating supply nipple. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a Charlottesville, VA home. Water coming from the bottom seam means the tank itself is done. No patch fixes that.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Charlottesville, VA

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. The tech reads the code and tests the thermopile and sensors before condemning the valve. Sometimes the valve really is bad. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. You get the old valve back if you want it.

    Pilot Light and Ignition Repair in Charlottesville

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. The tech pulls the pilot assembly and checks the thermocouple tip, the orifice and the igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Most trucks carry the common pilot kits.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Repair in Charlottesville, VA

    Restaurants and salons in Charlottesville, VA burn through hot water faster than any house, and a down heater stops the business. Most commercial tanks hold 75 to 100 gallons and run far higher BTU inputs than a home unit. We work on those larger atmospheric and power vent units, including the electronic controls, the burner manifolds and the piping that ties two tanks together on one supply. Every hour down matters. Repairs can often be booked before you open or after you close.

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Charlottesville

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. Air enters through a flame arrestor screen at the base that also keeps flame inside. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. We clean the screen and the chamber. A tripped thermal switch gets replaced.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Diagnosis in Charlottesville

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. That usually means a relief valve or a partly closed supply valve.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Charlottesville

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.
