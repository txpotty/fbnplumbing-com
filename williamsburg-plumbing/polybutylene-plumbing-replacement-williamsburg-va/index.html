    Why Polybutylene Fails in Williamsburg

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. Over the years, the pipe and its acetal fittings get brittle from the inside, then crack along the inner wall until one splits under normal pressure. Private wells serve many rural Virginia homes, and some well water carries iron, manganese, low pH or sulfur that calls for treatment. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Williamsburg

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. Uponor is our usual pick. Copper replacements use Type L instead of thin Type M, since the heavier wall lasts longer.

    Replacing a whole supply system takes special tools, code knowledge and real repipe experience. It takes skill and time. DIY mistakes can leave leaks hidden in walls, fail inspection or void the pipe warranty, which is why we recommend leaving it to licensed plumbers.

    Most policies do not pay for replacing old pipe, though they may cover sudden water damage. Certain insurers surcharge or refuse homes with poly.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper often goes further. Type L copper can last 50 years or more in friendly water, and either one is a large step up from polybutylene.

    Gray, blue or black plastic pipe marked PB2110 at the water heater or under sinks is the clearest sign. Age is another. A house from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe and a past fitting leak should be replaced before the next failure.

    Replacing an entire supply system requires routing, pressure testing and permits. A licensed crew finishes faster and provides warranties and documentation.
