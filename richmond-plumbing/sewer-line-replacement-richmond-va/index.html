    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Richmond

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Richmond, VA

    Most of our sewer work is single-family homes. Basements are common in northern and western parts of Virginia, while crawlspaces and slabs are more typical in the east and south.     Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Richmond

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. We open a trench along the old line, pull the broken pipe and lay new PVC on compacted bedding at a steady fall of an eighth to a quarter inch per foot. Sod gets cut and rolled aside first when the lawn allows it. The inspector sees open pipe. Backfill goes in compacted lifts so the trench line does not sink into a ditch a year later.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Richmond

    A winch pulls the bursting head. The head is slightly bigger than the old pipe, so it splits clay or cast iron outward into the soil while fused HDPE trails behind it in one continuous piece. Only two pits get dug. With no joints in the new pipe, roots have no way back in.

    How We Price Sewer Replacement in Richmond

    Length and depth drive the price. A forty-foot lateral four feet down in an open lawn costs a lot less than ninety feet under a driveway at eight feet, and we price both honestly.     Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Richmond

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. After cutting roots and jetting the line, we pull in a resin-filled liner and inflate it until it cures against the old pipe wall. Collapsed pipe will not work. Lining shines under finished basements and slabs. We say plainly whether your line qualifies.

    Pipe Bursting Under Driveways in Richmond, VA

    Concrete is expensive to replace. Bursting lets us run new HDPE under a driveway, patio or mature tree by pulling it through the path of the old pipe from a launch pit to a receiving pit. The HDPE is butt-fused on site into one long piece. A typical burst takes one working day. Restoration is two small holes instead of a sawcut slab.
