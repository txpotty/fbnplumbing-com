    Sump Pump Maintenance in Richmond, VA Ahead of Storm Season

    Float Switch Checks in Richmond, VA

    The float switch tells the pump when to start and stop, and a float with too little travel between on and off makes the pump short cycle dozens of times an hour. We watch the float through a full cycle. Next we adjust the on and off levels so each run is longer and the starts are fewer.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Service in Richmond, VA

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. We check the rod for bends and the stops for slipping. Bearings get a listen. Rattling or a high whine at startup usually means the motor is near the end.

    Pre-Storm Season Pump Testing in Richmond

    Mountain areas face flash flooding when heavy rain pours off steep slopes and into narrow valleys. A few weeks ahead of the wet season, we run a hose into the pit until the float trips and the pump starts. Then we time how fast it pumps out and listen to the bearings. That leaves time to replace a weak pump before the first big storm, not during it.

    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Richmond, VA

    Float trouble causes more no-start calls than anything else. A tethered float can tangle on the pump body or catch on the side of the basin. Vertical floats stick when mineral crust builds up on the rod. In tight pits where nothing has room to swing, we can switch you to an electronic sensor switch with no moving float at all.

    Annual Sump Pump Inspections in Richmond

    Once a year is the minimum. Each yearly visit covers the motor, the impeller, the float and the discharge line. Then we fill the pit and time how long the pump takes to empty it, because a slow drawdown is the earliest sign of a worn impeller or a partly blocked line.

    Emergency Sump Pump Diagnostics in Richmond, VA

    Grinding, clicking and a hum with no water moving each point to a different failure. Our plumber works from the outlet and cord to the float and then the motor. Often it is just a tripped GFCI or a hung float. A seized motor calls for a new pump, so we carry replacements and never leave the pit unprotected overnight.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Richmond, VA

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. We check the battery with a load tester, not just a voltmeter. Next, we cut power to the main pump and watch the backup clear the basin by itself. Alarms get tested too.

    Check Valve and Discharge Pipe Repair in Richmond, VA

    A bad check valve can nearly double how often the pump starts. Every startup puts heat into the motor. We swap worn valves for quiet, spring-loaded models and inspect the discharge pipe for cracks and loose couplings all the way to the exit point. The water should exit well away from the slab or foundation so it does not soak back toward the pit.

    Local Sump Pump Maintenance Near Me in Richmond, VA

    Our Richmond plumbers have serviced Zoeller, Liberty, Wayne and Little Giant pumps in all kinds of pits, from old fieldstone basements to new poured walls. The price is set before we pick up a tool.

    Water pools in predictable places around a house: the downspout corners, the low side of the yard and wherever the grade slopes back toward the wall. We look at all of that while we are outside checking the discharge. Small grading fixes help the pump.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Maintenance in Richmond

    A standard inspection and cleaning typically runs $125 to $250. Replacing a float switch or check valve typically adds $75 to $250 for parts and labor.

    Listen for grinding or rattling during a cycle, which usually means debris caught in the impeller or a bearing that is starting to fail. Constant running, a pump that will not kick on or a foul smell from the pit all call for a visit. Rust on the housing is a warning too.

    Even moderate hardness in Richmond can leave scale in water heaters over time, so regular flushing helps them run longer and more efficiently. In hard-water areas, scale builds up on the impeller, the float rod and the basin walls. Over a few years that crust slows the pump down and can lock a vertical float in place so the pump never starts. We scrape it off during maintenance.

    We work on Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant, Basement Watchdog, Liberty and most other residential brands. Common floats and seal kits ride on the truck.

    You can check that the pump is plugged in and the discharge outlet outside is not buried or blocked. A bucket of water shows whether it starts. What a homeowner check misses is amp draw, check valve wear and early float problems, which take a meter and pulling the pump to see.

    The check valve stops water in the discharge pipe from dropping back into the pit after the pump shuts off. Without it, the pump repeats its work. All that extra cycling shortens motor life and adds to the power bill.

    Grinding, rattling, nonstop running, a pump that will not start, rust on the housing or damp spots on the floor near the pit. Any one of those is worth a visit before the next storm.
