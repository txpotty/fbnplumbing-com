    Slab Leak Reroutes in Richmond

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. Rerouting one branch overhead and leaving the bad pipe in the slab is often cheaper than both a repipe and a jackhammer repair. It suits a first-time leak. After multiple leaks, we will show you why a full repipe may cost less over the next ten years.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Richmond, VA

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. For a typical house, copper often runs 20 to 40 percent above PEX, though some buyers and homeowners like the look and feel of metal pipe and are willing to pay for it. Both prices include permits, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Richmond, VA

    Private wells serve many rural Virginia homes, and some well water carries iron, manganese, low pH or sulfur that calls for treatment. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. In hard water homes, PEX-A is often the better pick since it resists scale, and a softener loop or filter can be added during the job. Adding the loop now costs less than later.

    Condo, Townhome and Multi-Family Repipes in Richmond

    Shared walls and HOA rules make repiping attached homes more involved. We coordinate with the HOA, schedule water shutoffs that may affect neighbors and protect adjoining units from dust and noise as much as we can. Property managers get phased schedules. Big buildings get repiped by stack or wing, which lets residents stay home.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Richmond

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. That is often the first sign. New PEX or copper stops the rust, restores pressure and ends leaks at the threads, which is where galvanized fails first. We disconnect and abandon the old pipe where removing it would mean tearing out finished walls.

    Drywall Patching and Restoration in Richmond, VA

    Repiping needs small openings in walls and ceilings to reach fixtures and pull pipe. Our crew cuts neat rectangles, covers nearby surfaces and patches each opening after the pressure test. We match the texture. We match knockdown, orange peel or smooth walls, so the patches are ready to prime and paint.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Richmond, VA

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. Brittle pipe walls and cracked fittings mean one polybutylene leak usually signals risk across the whole house. Full replacement is the fix. Many insurers ask about it.

    Repipe Costs Versus Leak Repairs in Richmond, VA

    A whole-home PEX repipe for an average house often runs $5,000 to $12,000, with copper costing more. A single slab leak repair can run $1,000 to $4,000 once access and restoration are included. Do the math. After two or three leaks, a repipe often pays for itself, and we give you a side-by-side comparison so you can make that call with real numbers in front of you.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX is cheaper. PEX bends, resists scale and handles freezes better, and copper is rigid, UV-tolerant and proven but pricier and vulnerable to aggressive water.

    Low pressure throughout the house, discolored water and frequent leaks are the main ones. Look at exposed pipe too. Green buildup on copper, rusty galvanized at the heater or gray pipe marked PB2110 are worth having checked, especially in homes over 40 years old.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. Uponor PEX-A is our usual PEX. Viega and Zurn PEX show up on some jobs, but we keep one brand throughout each house so the warranty holds.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. It helps at sale time. Swapping out polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized makes a home easier to insure and sell, especially with a permit record and transferable warranty.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It is not a weekend job. A DIY repipe can leave hidden leaks inside walls, fail inspection or void the pipe manufacturer's warranty, and any of those can cost more than hiring a licensed crew in the first place.

    Rusty or brown water, low pressure at several fixtures and repeated pinhole leaks are the classic signs. Noisy pipes can be another. Homes with galvanized, polybutylene or Kitec should plan a repipe before a failure does it for them, because those pipes fail system-wide.
