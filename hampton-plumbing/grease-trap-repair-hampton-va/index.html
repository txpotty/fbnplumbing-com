    Residential Grease Trap Repair in Hampton, VA

    Clogged Grease Trap Repair in Hampton

    Hardened grease in the inlet tee is the usual culprit when a trap backs up. We pump the trap down, then jet the inlet line with a 4,000 PSI unit until water runs free.     Leaking Grease Trap Repair in Hampton, VA

    A wet floor around an under-sink trap usually points to a failed lid gasket or a loose inlet coupling. Older steel bodies rust out along the bottom seams, too.     Baffle Repair and Replacement in Hampton

    Inside a concrete gravity interceptor the baffle is usually a poured or precast wall with a crossover opening. A crumbling wall lets grease slip into the second compartment and out the outlet. We can rebuild the wall with form work and a sulfate-resistant mix or set a polyethylene baffle panel, depending on the tank. Both require a full pump-out and a dry tank.

    Lid, Gasket and Riser Repair in Hampton, VA

    Outdoor risers take a beating from garbage trucks and the occasional forklift. A cracked riser lets surface water and grit into the interceptor. We replace damaged risers and set traffic-rated covers, H-20 rated where vehicles cross, and seal the riser joints with butyl rope so the tank stays tight. A light-duty lid in a drive aisle will break eventually.

    Damaged Baffle Repair in Hampton

    Baffles are the walls inside the trap that slow the water down long enough for grease to float and solids to sink. Once one cracks or falls loose, the trap is just a box that grease passes through. We pump it out and pull the old baffle. On plastic units we order the factory part for that model number. Steel traps with a rusted-out baffle may need a fabricated piece, and sometimes a new trap is the cheaper answer.

    Commercial Grease Interceptor Repair in Hampton, VA

    In-ground gravity interceptors run from about 500 gallons to several thousand, usually precast concrete with two compartments. Hydrogen sulfide from the waste eats the concrete above the waterline over time. We pump the tank, inspect the walls and the crossover, and repair spalled concrete with a cementitious patch or an epoxy liner rated for that service. Nobody goes in without a gas monitor, a tripod and a second person topside.
