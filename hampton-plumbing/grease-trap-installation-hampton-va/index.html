    Plastic and steel traps both wear out, just in different ways. Steel rusts at the seams and the baffle welds. Plastic cracks. We remove the old unit, set the new one in the same spot when it fits, and use no-hub couplings so future work is easier.

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer.     Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Hampton

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow.     Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Hampton

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. We quote in writing after a site visit, with the trap, the labor and the permit fees listed as separate lines.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. We register the unit with the maker so the factory warranty is on record. Keep your cleaning logs. Some manufacturers ask for proof of regular service before they honor a claim on a cracked body or a failed baffle.

    Pump-outs every couple of weeks on a trap that used to go two months is the clearest sign you have outgrown it or the baffles have failed. So is a sour grease smell near the floor sinks. Rust shows up too. We also look downstream, because grease in the outlet pipe means the trap is letting it through.

    That depends on the code your jurisdiction follows and the type of trap. Rules split, since hot water and detergent can carry grease past some indoor units. We check before we pipe it. Garbage disposers are usually not allowed to drain into a grease interceptor.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. Strictly, a grease trap is the small indoor unit rated in gallons per minute, and an interceptor is the large tank rated in gallons of capacity. Codes vary on the wording. Some plumbing codes call both of them interceptors and separate them as hydromechanical and gravity, so we use whatever terms your permit office uses.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. Automatic grease removal units are a pricier option for kitchens that fry all day. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Sinks are calculated by compartment volume and drain-down time, typically one or two minutes, and dish machines by the manufacturer's discharge rating. That gives the GPM figure. Hydromechanical traps are picked by that GPM rating, while gravity interceptors are sized by volume and retention time from the same data.

    It keeps cooking grease out of the public sewer. Inside the trap, warm wastewater slows and cools, so grease rises and stays put while water flows on. Without one, that grease hardens in the city mains and causes sewer overflows, and sewer authorities fine the kitchens they trace it to. Your own drain lines benefit too.
