    Electric Tankless for Guest Suites and Additions in Hampton, VA

    Guest suites, detached studios and additions often sit far from the main water heater. Long hot lines waste water and heat, so a nearby electric tankless is often the better choice. We size it for the space. There is nothing to vent.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Sizing and Selection in Hampton

    Sizing comes down to two numbers, flow rate and temperature rise. A 70 degree rise with a 27 kW unit gives roughly 2.6 GPM, enough for one shower and a sink. Warmer water means more flow. We check your panel and plumbing layout too.

    Rheem Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Hampton

    Rheem's RTEX line covers most of the whole-home electric tankless units we install. The RTEX-18 runs on two 40 amp breakers, while the RTEX-24 and RTEX-27 need three, so we match the model to your panel before ordering. Wall mounting is simple. We connect water, set temperature and test for leaks.

    Navien Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Hampton, VA

    Navien is best known for gas tankless, but FBN Plumbing installs whatever brand fits your home in Hampton, VA. We check the model against your panel and flow, then install it to spec to protect the warranty. Size beats brand. Code governs the rest.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Upgrades in Hampton, VA

    Upgrading from a 50 gallon electric tank to tankless is mostly an electrical project. The old tank ran on a single 30 amp circuit, and the new unit may need two or three 40 amp circuits, which is where most of the time and cost go. Plumbing is the easy part. Old tank removal is included.

    Residential Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Hampton

    Residential electric tankless installs start with counting fixtures that run at the same time. A household running two showers at once in cold weather usually needs a 27 kW unit on three 40 amp breakers. Smaller homes need less. We install Rheem, EcoSmart and Stiebel Eltron.

    Electric Tankless Replacement and Upgrades in Hampton

    Older electric tankless units start to struggle when elements burn out or flow sensors fail. We replace aging units with newer models that have better flow control and sometimes higher kilowatt ratings, and we check the wiring and the plumbing while the old unit is off the wall. Error codes tell a story. Hot water steadies out.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless Electric in Hampton

    Switching from tank to tankless electric usually takes panel work and some re-piping. The higher amperage often means new breakers, heavier wire and sometimes a service upgrade, and the water lines get moved to meet the unit on the wall. We coordinate the electrician. You get floor space back.
