    Drain Cleaning and Main Line Backups in Hampton

    Slab Leak Location and Repair in Hampton

    Slab leaks hide under concrete until something gives them away. Expansive clays in some areas swell when wet and shrink when dry, which can crack foundations and shift buried sewer lines. We locate them with acoustic listening gear and a thermal camera, so the only concrete we break is the small patch right over the leak, not half the kitchen floor. Then you pick the fix. Some leaks get a spot repair, others a reroute overhead that skips the slab entirely.

    Once a year is a good rule. In homes with hard water, sediment builds faster and the tank may need flushing twice a year, and a yearly check lets us test the relief valve, look at the anode rod and catch a weeping fitting before it rusts through the jacket. An anode rod often wears out in three to five years.

    Emergencies go to the top of the board. We aim to have a plumber at the house within a few hours, and for a burst pipe or sewage backup we will walk you through closing the main shutoff on the phone so the damage stops while we drive. Grab towels and a bucket while you wait.

    Watch for drips that will not stop, slow drains, banging or whistling pipes, a sudden drop in pressure, discolored water and wet spots with no obvious source. Each one is a symptom of something that will get worse. Do not ignore them.

    Our plumbers install and service Moen, Delta, Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Rheem, Bradford White and Navien, among others. We will tell you which ones hold up in your water, since cartridges in some brands handle hard water better than others. Some cost more but last longer.

    Swapping a showerhead or a toilet flapper is fine for most people. Gas lines, water heaters and anything behind a wall belong with a licensed plumber, for safety and because those jobs usually need a permit and an inspection that a homeowner repair can fail. Thread tape is not a gas repair.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Grease out of the drains and a yearly pressure test help too, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. An annual check finds small issues early.
