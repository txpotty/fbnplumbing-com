    Keep grease, coffee grounds and wipes out of drains. Hair strainers in tubs and showers help. Run hot water after using the disposal, and scrape plates into the trash before rinsing so less food goes down the kitchen line. Older lines with roots need scheduled rooting every 12 to 24 months.

    Yes, most rooter jobs come with a limited guarantee. The terms depend on the line and what we found, and they are written on your estimate. Roots are the exception.     Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go many years without it. A camera look can set the timing. How quickly roots return after the last cut tells us if yearly or every-other-year service fits your line.

    Most single-drain clogs take 1 to 3 hours from arrival to flow test. A root-packed main or a job that adds jetting can take most of a day. Access makes the biggest difference. A buried or missing cleanout means pulling a toilet first, which adds about an hour.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They also burn skin. A cable takes the clog out instead of melting a narrow path through it, which keeps the drain open longer.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. It works well on hair, wipes and roots.
