    Appliance Connector Replacement in Suffolk

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. If your range or water heater still has one, it should come out. We see them weekly. The replacement is a coated stainless connector sized for the appliance, with a new ball valve and a sediment trap where one is required. Bubble testing follows before the burner is lit.

    Interior Gas Pipe Repair in Suffolk, VA

    Inside the house, gas leaks hide at unions in the basement and at the threaded nipple behind the furnace, places nobody looks. Testing runs from the meter inward, one section at a time. Drywall stays intact when possible. Rerouting with CSST past a leak in a finished wall is often cheaper than cutting and patching drywall. Nothing is turned back on until the gauge holds steady.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Suffolk, VA

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Every time somebody slides the stove out to mop, the connector takes another bend. Valves weep at the stem. The fix is usually a new coated connector and a new ball valve, plus a drip leg if the appliance lacks one. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is lit again.

    Hissing at the meter, a sulfur smell in the kitchen, a dead patch of lawn or a carbon monoxide alarm all mean the same thing: get everyone outside before you touch anything. Call the utility from outside, not from inside the house. FBN Plumbing runs emergency gas calls in Suffolk, VA around the clock. After the utility closes the meter, we cut off the bad branch, fix it and test it. Unaffected appliances usually come back the same day.

    Underground Gas Line Repair in Suffolk

    Yard lines run from the meter to the house, and often on to a detached garage, a pool heater, a grill or an outdoor kitchen. Buried steel loses its coating to rocks in the backfill and then rusts through. Roots and settling do damage too. After an 811 locate, we excavate, cut out the failed run and lay polyethylene with a tracer wire beside it. Sand goes around the new pipe before backfill, per Suffolk trench rules.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Suffolk

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. We check regulator lockup and vent lines along with the joints. Rooftop units leak too. If a shutdown would cost you a dinner rush, we valve off just the bad branch and leave the rest live. Staff get a short briefing on the shutoff locations.

    Corrugated Stainless Steel Tubing Repair in Suffolk, VA

    CSST is the yellow-jacketed flexible tubing used in many newer homes, and it can be damaged by a nearby lightning strike if it was never bonded. Bonding means a 6 AWG copper wire from the gas piping to the electrical grounding system. Plenty of older installs lack it. We repair CSST only with the matching brand's fittings, because TracPipe and Gastite parts do not interchange. A splice that skips that rule fails inspection.
