    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Leesburg

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. No water, low pressure or a pump that hums without moving water are the usual signs one has failed. We test the motor from the surface first. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Leesburg

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. FBN Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Leesburg, VA well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Pressure Tank Problems in Leesburg

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. Usually the bladder has failed or the tank has lost its air charge. We check the precharge with the tank drained. Set it 2 psi under cut-in, which means about 38 psi for a 40/60 switch.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in Leesburg

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. These pumps can also leak at the shaft seal or wear out an impeller. The pump gets reprimed and resealed, and the foot valve and suction line get checked, because a single air leak will undo the work. Everything is above ground.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Leesburg

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Leesburg, VA

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Mountain areas face flash flooding when heavy rain pours off steep slopes and into narrow valleys. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. Arrestors are cheap.
