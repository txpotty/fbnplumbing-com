    Gas Tankless Water Heater Repair in Leesburg

    Pressure at full fire matters on a gas tankless burner, not just at the moment it lights.     Rinnai Error Code Repair in Leesburg

    Rinnai owners usually call about a code 10, which points to an air supply or exhaust blockage. We check the vent terminal for bird nests and dryer lint, then pull the front cover to inspect the intake screen and the combustion fan. A code 10 can also come from a vent run that is too long for the model. We measure.

    Electric Tankless Element Repair in Leesburg, VA

    Electric units fail in their own way. Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart models use two to four heating elements in separate chambers, and one burned element means weak heat at high flow, while a failed flow switch means no heat at all. We check each element's resistance, measure voltage under load and check amperage on each leg, then confirm the panel and wire size match the unit's rating.

    Noritz heaters hold up well, but their flow sensors and vent connections still wear out. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake.     Ignition Failure Repair in Leesburg, VA

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss.     Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Leesburg

    Descaling usually runs $175 to $350. Part repairs mostly fall between $175 and $600, and a heat exchanger replacement can top $800 when the warranty has run out. The quote comes before the wrench.

    We service Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Bosch and AO Smith gas units, plus Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart electrics. Parts for discontinued brands take longer to find.

    Cleaning the inlet filter and wiping down the intake screen are fair homeowner jobs. Opening the sealed burner compartment is not, because a misaligned gasket or loose gas fitting there can leak exhaust or gas into the house, and many warranties are voided by unlicensed work. That work belongs to a licensed plumber.

    You can clean the inlet water filter and the air intake screen on most models with the manual open beside you. Gas valves, igniters and anything behind the sealed combustion cover should go to a licensed plumber, and DIY work there can void the manufacturer warranty. Leave the burner alone.

    Plan on one to three hours for a common fix like a flame rod or flow sensor. If the part is not on the truck, we usually return within a few business days.

    Hard water drops scale inside the heat exchanger every time it heats, and those passages are small. Skip the yearly flush and the unit runs hotter, loses output at high flow and eventually locks out on an overheat or scale code, and that can turn a routine descale into an exchanger replacement. The flush takes about an hour.

    Watch for hot water that takes longer to show up, a burner that cycles on and off, water around the base, or a code on the display. A drip inside the cabinet rusts parts fast.

    Scale is first by a wide margin. Next on the list are blocked air intakes, dirty flame rods and failed flow sensors, with control boards and fan motors showing up less often, usually on units past ten years old. Most of that list can be prevented.
