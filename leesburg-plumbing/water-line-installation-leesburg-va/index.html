    This is a job for a licensed plumber. You are dealing with excavation, pipe sizing and code rules, plus coordination with the water utility at the meter, and a poor install can lead to leaks, water damage and contamination of your drinking water. It is not worth the risk.

    Type K copper, PEX and HDPE are all approved for buried service. The plastic options flex and need fewer joints, while copper gives you rigidity and puncture resistance.

    Low pressure everywhere in the house points at the main, not one fixture. Rusty water and a wet strip in the lawn are other clues, especially along the path between the meter and the house, and a bill that jumps without a reason usually confirms it. That is how old mains fail.

    Expansive clays in some areas expand when wet and shrink when dry, which can crack foundations and shift buried sewer lines. Soil that shifts and settles puts steady stress on buried pipe.
