    Liberty Pumps Installation in Leesburg, VA

    Liberty builds quiet, well-made submersible pumps with good switch options. Their 250 and 280 series cover most residential lifts, and the vertical float version fits narrow pits where a wide tethered float would rub. Replacement switches are easy to get. A small weep hole in the discharge pipe keeps the pump from air locking.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Leesburg, VA

    A dead pump during a storm means the water is already climbing. We carry common pumps on the truck. Figure on two hours for a typical swap. We test it with a full pit before leaving.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Leesburg, VA

    Backup systems we install use sealed AGM batteries that do not need water. A smart charger keeps the battery topped off and sounds an alarm if the battery weakens, the primary fails or the water rises past the backup float. Batteries need replacing every few years. Finished basements get the most out of them.

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Leesburg

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi models send a text too. Rentals need these. We add them to new installs and to pumps already in place.

    New Sump Pit Installation in Leesburg

    No pit yet? We cut one at the low corner. Washed gravel goes around the basin so water can reach it. Then we patch the slab. Last come the pump, the lid and the discharge line.

    Commercial Sump Pump Solutions in Leesburg

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and loading dock drains need pumps rated for more volume. We install duplex systems with alternating pumps. Downtime costs money. We plan the install around your business hours.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Leesburg

    Most Leesburg, VA sump pump jobs land between $600 and $1,800. That covers the pump, the check valve and labor. New pits cost more. We write it all down before work starts.

    Most last seven to ten years. Pumps that sit idle for long stretches can fail sooner, so test yours a few times a year.

    Most standard installs take three to six hours. If we have to cut the slab, dig a new pit and patch the concrete, plan on most of a day, with the patch curing overnight. Swaps go faster.

    Submersibles are quieter and stronger, so they suit heavy use. Pedestal pumps cost less and are easy to service, though they are louder.

    Have it cleaned and inspected once a year, ideally before the rainy season. We check the float, clean the pit and test the discharge. Small fixes prevent big floods.

    A straight replacement often needs no permit. We let you know if your job needs one, usually only for new pits or wiring.

    Strange noises, nonstop running and visible rust are the usual signs. A quick test before the rainy season catches most problems.

    Grinding, rattling or a pump that runs with no water in the pit are all warning signs. Visible rust, a float that sticks or a pump that cycles every minute during dry weather usually means the end is near. A flooded basement settles it.
