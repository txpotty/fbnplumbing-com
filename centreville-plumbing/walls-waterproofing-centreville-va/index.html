    Drainage System Integration in Centreville, VA

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Exterior footing drains, interior drain tile and surface French drains all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a downspout emptying right at the foundation. We extend downspouts at least four feet from the wall.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Centreville, VA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Humid summers and moderate winters are typical for most of Virginia, though mountain areas see colder temperatures and longer heating seasons. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then coat the face with a masonry waterproofing product that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. Those weep holes empty into the interior drain if the house has one.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Centreville

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that shows the cool wet spot, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. The bad fitting or section of pipe gets replaced. We pull wet insulation and run fans until the cavity reads dry.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Centreville, VA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Housing in Virginia spans centuries, from historic homes with cast iron and galvanized pipe to newer subdivisions plumbed with PEX. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. Sealing soft old brick with a rigid coating traps water inside it and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with a new interior drain. We repoint failed mortar joints before sealing.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Centreville

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Soils in Virginia range from sandy ground with a high water table near the coast to dense clay and rocky karst farther inland. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Centreville, VA

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and run it to a sump basin so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.
