    Simple buried pipe repairs usually run $500 to $2,500. Big jobs climb higher. Extensive trenchless sewer replacements can be $5,000 to $15,000 or more, depending on where the pipe runs, how bad the damage is and the method used to fix it. We quote after inspection.

    Check the meter with every fixture off. Look at the leak indicator. If it moves, water is going somewhere, and a wet or sinking spot in the yard or a hissing sound near the meter box usually points to the buried service. We can confirm it fast.

    Most point repairs take a few hours to a day. Full lines run longer. A full service line replacement typically takes one to two days, depending on length, depth and how much of the route can be done with a pull-through method. We give you the timeline first.

    Small repairs often do not. Full lines typically need one. Full water service or sewer line replacement usually requires a permit, and any work in the public right-of-way always needs coordination with the city. We handle it when required.

    Acoustic ground microphones pick up the sound of escaping water. We also use tracer gas. Hydrogen-nitrogen gas pushed into the line rises through the soil at the leak, and electronic locators trace the exact pipe route so we know where to listen. The mark is usually very close.

    Our labor warranty is written into the quote. Length depends on the job. A spot repair and a full service replacement carry different coverage since each job uses different methods and parts. Pipe makers add their own coverage.
