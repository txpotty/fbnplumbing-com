    Slab Leak Detection and Repair in Fredericksburg

    Slab leaks hide under concrete until something gives them away. Basements are common in northern and western parts of Virginia, while crawlspaces and slabs are more typical in the east and south. We locate them with acoustic listening gear and a thermal camera, so the only concrete we break is the small patch right over the leak, not half the kitchen floor. Then you pick the fix. Options include a spot repair through the slab or a reroute through the attic.

    Faucet and Fixture Repair in Fredericksburg

    Most dripping faucets need a cartridge, not a new faucet. We carry cartridges for Moen, Delta, Kohler and Pfister, and when you do want new fixtures we install them, connect new braided supply lines, and check the angle stops under the sink while we are there. Old stops fail when touched.

    Emergency Plumbing Repairs in Fredericksburg

    Sewage in the tub at night counts as an emergency. The plumber shows up with a drain machine and a shop vac, clears the line, and checks whether the backup came from a simple clog or a main line that needs a camera and a real repair. You get both answers before we leave.

    Gas Lines for New Appliances in Fredericksburg, VA

    Switching from an electric range to gas means a new line and a shutoff. We size the pipe for the appliance's BTU load, run it in black iron or CSST, bond it where the code requires and pressure-test before the gas company or inspector signs off on the work. Then we light it and check the flame.

    Leak Repair and Slab Leak Service in Fredericksburg

    On a slab foundation, a supply leak can run for weeks unseen. The usual clues are a warm patch of floor, a meter dial moving with everything off, or a sudden jump in the water bill. Nothing gets quoted until the leak is pinpointed. Fixes range from a direct repair to rerouting the line overhead or a full repipe when leaks keep coming back.

    Water Heater Repair and Installation in Fredericksburg

    A water heater works hardest in winter, when incoming water is coldest. We work on gas, electric, tank and tankless heaters from Rheem and Bradford White, and the usual culprits are a heavy layer of sediment or a thermocouple that has quit reading the pilot. No hot water is often a quick part swap. New units get an expansion tank and pan when the code requires it.
