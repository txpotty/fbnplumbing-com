    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Portsmouth, VA

    Storms knock out power at the exact moment the pit is filling fastest. A battery backup pump sits beside the primary and runs on its own float. Most run five to seven hours on a full charge. We mount the charger on the wall, off the floor.

    Primary Sump Pump Installation in Portsmouth

    A primary sump pump is the one doing the daily work, kicking on every time the float rises in the pit after rain or snowmelt. Sizing comes from two measurements we take on site, the pit diameter and the vertical lift to the discharge. Most homes need 1/3 or 1/2 HP. A 1-1/2 inch discharge line handles the flow from either size without straining the motor.

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Portsmouth

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi monitors go further and send a phone alert when the pump runs too long, the power drops or the water climbs past a set level. Rentals need these. They can go on a new pump or one that is already working.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Portsmouth

    Submersible pumps live at the bottom of the pit with a sealed, oil-cooled motor. They run quieter and move more water. The pit lid sits flush. Each install gets a sealed lid and a grommet around the cord to keep out radon and odor.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Portsmouth, VA

    Backup systems we install use sealed AGM batteries that do not need water. The charger keeps the battery topped off. Batteries need replacing every few years. They matter most below finished rooms.

    Sump Pump Installation Cost in Portsmouth, VA

    Price depends mostly on whether a pit already exists. A swap in an existing pit is the low end. Battery backups and alarms add to the total. Your written quote shows each option and its price.

    Combination Sump Pump Systems in Portsmouth

    Combination units pair a primary pump and a battery backup on one pre-plumbed discharge assembly. They fit pits where two separate pumps would crowd each other. Install time is shorter too. They suit homes that want backup protection but do not want to enlarge the pit.

    Exterior Drainage and Yard Sump Pumps in Portsmouth

    Sunken patios, below-grade driveways and deep window wells collect water with nowhere to go. We install exterior basins with pumps for those spots. Lots that slope toward the house need this most. The outlet sits well clear of the foundation and the property line.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Portsmouth

    A pump swap in an existing pit typically costs $600 to $1,500. New pits, with saw cutting, excavation and a concrete patch, run about $1,500 to $4,000. Add $500 to $1,500 for a backup.

    Plenty of cities require a plumbing permit for new sump pump installs or major replacements. FBN Plumbing handles the permit in Portsmouth, VA so the work passes inspection.

    Seven to ten years is typical. Cast iron pumps with mechanical switches tend to outlast plastic ones, and a pump that rests longer between cycles lasts longer. Yearly cleaning stretches it.

    A submersible sits in the pit under water, so it runs quieter and takes no floor space. The pedestal type mounts the motor on a column above the water, which makes it easier to reach for service and usually cheaper to buy, though you will hear it. Either can do the job. We help you pick based on your basement.

    For some homeowners, swapping a pump in a working pit is doable. New pits, discharge routing and electrical changes are better left to a plumber.

    Most homeowners are better off hiring it out. An undersized pump, a backward check valve or a discharge that dumps against the foundation can cause the very flooding you were trying to stop. Safety matters here.

    Three to five hours covers most standard jobs. Cutting a new pit or rerouting the electrical can turn it into an all-day job, especially when we hit rock or an old footing under the slab. We give you a time estimate up front.

    Discharge should end at least ten feet from the foundation, on ground that slopes away. Most cities do not allow sump water in the sanitary sewer.
