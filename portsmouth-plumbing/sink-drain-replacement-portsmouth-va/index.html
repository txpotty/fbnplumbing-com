    Pop-Up Drain Replacement in Portsmouth

    Lift-rod pop-ups are the most common bath drain. We pull the old body, clean the flange seat down to bare porcelain, reset the new flange in putty or silicone, and adjust the clevis so the lift rod opens and closes the stopper with one easy motion. Finish matches the faucet. Overflow-style drains are standard.

    Push-Button Drain Installation in Portsmouth, VA

    Push-button drains get rid of the lift rod. They open and close with a press on the cap, and we check that the sink has an overflow before choosing a slotted body, since a slotted drain in a bowl without an overflow leaks out the slots. They clean easier too. Hair still collects, so pull the cap now and then.

    Code Updates During Drain Replacement in Portsmouth

    Old drains sometimes break current rules. S-traps and drum traps were common long ago, and replacement brings them up to a vented P-trap. An AAV may be allowed where a vent is missing. Local rules decide.

    Kitchen Drain and Disposal Tie-In in Portsmouth

    Disposals change the kitchen drain. The outlet on the disposal has to sit above the trap inlet, the dishwasher hose needs a high loop or an air gap, and the trap has to line up without the slip joints pulling sideways. We set them all at once. Then the disposal runs with water while we watch.

    Corroded Drain Pipe Replacement in Portsmouth, VA

    Galvanized drain arms rust from the inside. An old steel stub can crumble at the threads, so we cut it back and join new PVC with a shielded coupling. We handle cast iron the same way. Sometimes that means opening the wall.

    Utility Sink Drain Replacement in Portsmouth

    Laundry tubs see lint and paint water. Lint and grit wear out thin utility tub drains and pack the trap so it never drains right. We fit a 1-1/2 inch trap with a cleanout. A lint trap on the washer hose helps.

    Utility and Basement Sink Drain Replacement in Portsmouth

    Basement sinks often drain to a pump. If your laundry or utility sink sits below the sewer line, its drain runs to a sink pump or a sewage ejector, and we check the check valve and the discharge line when we replace the trap. Pump trouble is easy to miss. A gurgling pit is a clue.

    Kitchen Sink Drain Replacement in Portsmouth

    Kitchen drains start failing at the basket strainer. Old strainer putty dries out and the locknut rusts, so water runs down the tailpiece and looks like a trap leak. We replace the strainer and trap together. Disposal sinks get a new flange instead.
