    Replacing Fleck Water Softeners in Portsmouth, VA

    Fleck control valves, the 5600SXT and 9100SXT in particular, are on a large share of the softeners we see in Virginia homes. An old Fleck with discontinued parts usually gets replaced, valve and tank together. Current Fleck valves fit standard tanks.

    Salt-Free Water Conditioner Replacement in Portsmouth, VA

    Salt-free conditioners, sometimes sold as descalers, change how hardness minerals behave without removing them, so the water still tests hard. What they do well is keep scale off pipe walls and heater elements. They need no drain line. Swapping to a salt-based unit is an option too.

    Clack Water Softener Replacement in Portsmouth

    Clack WS1 valves are easy to program and easy to rebuild, which is why a lot of water treatment shops have switched to them. A sound tank can keep its resin and get a new valve. Or the whole system.

    Upsizing a Whole-Home Softener in Portsmouth

    Undersized softeners are common in homes that added a bathroom or a couple of teenagers since the original system went in. What worked for three people can run out of capacity in a few days with five. Sizing is based on current usage.

    Salt-Based Water Softener Replacement in Portsmouth

    Salt-based softeners pull calcium and magnesium out of the water by trading them for sodium on a bed of resin beads, which is the only method that actually makes water soft. Old clock-timer units come out and metered valves go in. You will buy less salt. We set the brine refill to your hardness so the system is not dumping salt down the drain every few days.

    Softener Failure Diagnosis in Portsmouth, VA

    A clogged injector, a dead motor or a wrong setting can make a good softener look finished. We check those first and test hardness before and after the unit, so you know whether it is softening at all. Discontinued valves usually mean replacement.

    Resin Tank and Media Replacement in Portsmouth, VA

    Resin beads break down after 10 to 15 years, faster on chlorinated city water, and the softener keeps regenerating while the water slowly turns hard again. Rebedding with new resin can be cheaper than a new system. It depends on the valve. New resin under a tired valve rarely lasts.

    Salt-Based Softener Swaps in Portsmouth, VA

    Swapping a salt-based softener in Virginia homes means disconnecting the old resin and brine tanks, cutting in new fittings if the loop is corroded, and setting the new valve. We pressurize and check every joint. The drain line gets an air gap.
