    Emergency Basement Leak Repair in Portsmouth, VA

    A pipe that lets go in a basement can put an inch of water on the floor in under an hour. First, close the main water shutoff. Then call us, and a plumber will come out to stop the leak, repair the pipe, and get water back on so you can start drying the space. We can point you to a drying company too.

    Interior Drainage Systems in Portsmouth

    Where digging outside is not practical, an interior drain system does the same job from inside. We cut the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and slope it to a new or existing sump pit before patching the floor with fresh concrete. The wall gets a dimpled membrane. Water then runs behind it into the channel.

    Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Portsmouth

    A sump pump sits in a pit below the slab and kicks on when a float rises. Stuck floats, worn motors and failed check valves cause most of the service calls we run on sumps. We test yours with a bucket. A battery backup is worth adding where power drops in storms.

    Hydrostatic Pressure Management in Portsmouth, VA

    Water pressing against the outside of a basement wall works into the weakest spot, usually the cove joint where wall meets floor. Saturated ground after a storm can push water into basements and back up sewer lines, making backwater valves and sump pumps worthwhile. We relieve it with a perimeter channel at the cove joint that carries water to a sump pit. No pressure, no seepage.

    After-Hours Basement Leak Service in Portsmouth, VA

    Basement leaks rarely wait for business hours. A water heater tank can split in the middle of the night. Our on-call plumbers carry repair couplings, shutoff valves and a wet vac, so the first visit usually ends with the leak stopped and the water back on in the rest of the house.

    New Sump Pump Installation in Portsmouth

    Installing a new sump means breaking out a section of slab, setting a lined pit, and running the discharge out through the rim joist. Most of our installs get a cast iron pump, a check valve and 1-1/2 inch discharge. Discharge cannot go into the sewer in most places. It has to discharge outside, far enough from the foundation that it does not cycle back into the pit, often ten feet or more.
