    Repiping Work We Handle in Virginia Beach, VA

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Virginia Beach, VA

    PEX tubing resists freezing damage, corrosion and scale better than most metal pipe. It is quieter too. Flexible PEX soaks up some of the pressure spike behind water hammer, and its long fitting-free runs leave fewer weak points. Attic PEX gets kept away from flues and out of UV.

    Emergency Pipe Failures and Repipes in Virginia Beach

    Sometimes a house reaches the repipe point all at once, with a burst line and two more leaks right behind it. We stop the active leak first, make temporary repairs that get water back on and then plan the full repipe so you are not rushed into it. That gives you time. Your policy may pay for water damage even if not for the new pipe, so we take photos of everything we find.

    Residential Repiping Plans in Virginia Beach

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through. We count fixtures, check attic and crawlspace access, look at where the main comes in and ask about plans for future remodels before we draw a route. Then we size the lines. Bigger homes may need a 1-inch trunk so the shower does not drop when the dishwasher fills.

    Commercial Repiping in Virginia Beach, VA

    Commercial buildings in Virginia Beach, VA often have larger pipe, risers that serve several floors and tenants who cannot lose water for a full day. We phase commercial repipes by floor, suite or riser so most of the building stays in service while we work. Night work is possible. We use Type L copper or commercial-grade PEX sized from fixture units and installed to commercial code.

    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in Virginia Beach

    Copper is the traditional choice, and Type L copper has a thicker wall than the Type M that went into many older homes. It holds up well, does not mind sunlight in an exposed run and has a long track record behind it. It costs more. Our plumbers solder joints with lead-free solder or use press fittings, support the pipe at the spacing the code calls for and pressure test the system before any wall is closed.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Virginia Beach

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. Rerouting one branch overhead and leaving the bad pipe in the slab is often cheaper than both a repipe and a jackhammer repair. This works well for a first leak. If the house has had several leaks, we will walk through why a full repipe usually costs less over time.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Virginia Beach

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Many homes built from the 1980s through the early 2000s have one or the other. The pipe at the water heater usually tells us which one you have, and we replace the full system so the home is easier to insure and to sell.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Virginia Beach, VA

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Rust-colored water in the morning, poor pressure upstairs and dripping threaded joints all point to failing galvanized. We replace it with PEX or copper. Owners usually notice stronger flow the first day.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Virginia Beach

    Most whole-home repipes in Virginia Beach, VA fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. The final number depends on square footage, how many fixtures need new lines, whether you choose PEX or copper and how much drywall has to be opened and patched along the route. We quote it in writing.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX costs less. PEX bends, resists scale and handles freezes better, and copper is rigid, UV-tolerant and proven but pricier and vulnerable to aggressive water.

    Most repipes in Virginia Beach, VA take three to five working days for an average house. Big houses take more time. Copper takes a little longer than PEX because every turn needs a fitting, and the drywall patching usually adds a final day after the plumbing passes inspection.

    Yes, a whole-home repipe in Virginia Beach, VA usually needs a plumbing permit. The permit is on us. We file it, book the inspection while walls are open and close it out at the end, which puts the work on record.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Most are about the size of a sheet of paper. We cut clean squares at fixtures and along the route, patch every one after the inspection and match the existing texture, so the walls are ready to paint when we finish.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. Buyers notice it. Replacing polybutylene, Kitec or old galvanized makes a home easier to insure and easier to sell, and a permit record plus a transferable warranty gives the next owner proof the work was done.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It takes skill and time. A DIY repipe can leave hidden leaks inside walls, fail inspection or void the pipe manufacturer's warranty, and any of those can cost more than hiring a licensed crew in the first place.
