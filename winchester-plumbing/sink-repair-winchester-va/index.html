    Outdoor and Utility Sink Repair in Winchester

    Outdoor kitchen sinks and garage utility tubs sit in the weather.     Office and Restaurant Sink Repair in Winchester

    Office breakroom sinks get abused in ways a home sink never sees. Coffee grounds and paper towels go down all day, and nobody checks the trap until water is on the carpet. We clear it and add a strainer. For restaurants, we keep three-bowl sinks running between the lunch and dinner rush.

    Pop-Up Stopper Repair in Winchester, VA

    Stoppers that will not hold water have a linkage problem. Either the clevis slipped on the lift rod or hair has wrapped the stopper so it will not seat. Cleaning fixes half of them. The other half get a new pop-up drain in a matching finish.

    Low Water Pressure at the Sink in Winchester, VA

    Unscrew the aerator before anything else. Most single-faucet pressure problems are a packed aerator screen, and after that we look at the cartridge and the angle stop for debris. Whole-house low pressure is another call. We test the pressure regulator in that case.

    After-Hours Sink Leak Repair in Winchester, VA

    Leaks do not keep office hours. A burst supply line can dump gallons a minute, and we answer at night and on weekends with a stocked truck. Close the angle stops while you wait. If those will not hold, use the main.

    Leaking P-Trap Fix in Winchester

    Check the slip nuts first. Plastic slip nuts crack when they are overtightened with pliers, and the beveled washers inside them flatten until water seeps out at every joint along the trap. The whole trap gets replaced as a kit. A 1-1/4 inch kit for bath sinks, 1-1/2 inch for kitchens.

    Kitchen Sink Repair in Winchester

    Kitchen sinks take the most abuse in the house. Strainers lose their putty seal, sprayer hoses split inside the cabinet, and disposal flanges vibrate loose over the years. The wet part is the one we fix. A cracked composite or porcelain bowl is a replacement, and we will say so.

    P-Trap Repair and Replacement in Winchester, VA

    The trap holds a plug of water that keeps sewer gas out of the room. Most codes want 2 to 4 inches of trap seal, and a leaking or cut-down trap can lose it and let sewer odor into the house. Bad traps get a new matched kit. Chrome traps that have rusted thin get swapped for PVC or brass.
