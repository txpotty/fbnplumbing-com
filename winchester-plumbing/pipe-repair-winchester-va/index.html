    Pinhole Leak Repair in Winchester

    Pinhole leaks form when the copper wall thins from the inside. Water quality is often behind it. High velocity, poor grounding and aggressive water all speed it up, and the leak starts as a tiny drip that stains drywall or a ceiling, so we cut out the affected section and replace it with new copper or a PEX transition. More pinholes often follow.

    PEX and Copper Pipe Repair in Winchester, VA

    PEX and copper fail in different ways. Copper pins. Copper thins from the inside and develops pinholes, while PEX usually fails at fittings or where it was kinked during installation, so the truck carries tools and fittings for both. We test before closing up.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Pipe Repair in Winchester

    Minor leak repairs usually run $150 to $500. Hidden leaks run higher. Burst pipes inside finished walls or slab leaks can reach $1,000 to $4,000, with location, access and material driving most of the price. You get a written number after locating.

    A clamp or tape can stop a drip for a day. Temporary fixes fail. Pressurized supply lines need a proper fitting, and a DIY patch that lets go while you are at work can flood a room in the time it takes to drive home. Use it only until a plumber arrives.

    Stains on walls or ceilings are the most obvious sign. So does a damp odor. A drop in pressure, the sound of running water when nothing is on, a bill that jumps for no reason, or a meter that moves with every fixture off all mean there is a leak somewhere in the system. Brown water means corrosion.

    A simple accessible leak can be fixed in an hour or two. Hidden bursts run longer. Replacing a section of a main line or fixing a burst pipe hidden in a wall could take several hours or a full day, depending on access and materials. You get a timeline first.

    Fixing a small leak usually does not. Repipes often do. Larger projects in Winchester like extensive repiping, relocating a main water line or repairing a sewer line usually require a permit, which allows an inspection before anything is covered. The permit is on us.

    Corrosion from the inside of the pipe. Water chemistry drives it. High velocity, excess pressure and stray electrical current from poor grounding wear the pipe from within until it pins through. Private wells serve many rural Virginia homes, and some well water carries iron, manganese, low pH or sulfur that calls for treatment. One often means more.

    Policies often cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe. Worn pipe is usually excluded. The cost of fixing old or corroded pipe is usually yours, but the resulting damage to the house may be claimable. Check with your agent. We provide documentation.
