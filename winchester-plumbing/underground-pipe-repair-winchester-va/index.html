    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Winchester

    A single repair on a buried water line typically costs $800 to $2,500 including locating and excavation. A new service runs higher. Main water service replacement typically runs $2,500 to $6,500, while sewer lateral repairs usually fall between $1,500 and $5,000 based on depth. You get the price on paper.

    Wet spots or extra-green grass are the classic signs. The water bill often tells you first. A sudden drop in pressure, a sewage or sulfur smell in the yard, or gurgling drains may mean a broken pipe underground on the supply or drain side. A turning meter confirms a leak.

    It gets worse and more expensive. Soil washes out. A leaking service can undermine a driveway, soak a foundation, grow mold in a crawlspace and run up the water bill every month, and a cracked sewer lateral can let sewage into the soil around your home. Early repair costs less.

    We use PEX from major manufacturers like Uponor and Viega. Everything is code-listed. PVC and ABS products meet the ASTM specs for buried water or drain service, while every fitting we bury is listed for underground use. No off-brand fittings.

    Yes, every common material. Modern and legacy pipe. PVC, ABS, cast iron, copper, PEX, clay, galvanized and polybutylene all come up, and each one needs its own transition fitting when we join new pipe to the old. Common fittings ride on the truck.

    Water lines get PEX or Type K copper. Both are burial rated. Sewer lines get Schedule 40 PVC, and the final choice depends on the existing pipe, the soil and what your local code allows. Couplings get sized to both pipes.

    A spot repair is usually a few hours. Trenchless jobs take about a day. A full sewer replacement with excavation can take two to five days, with footage, depth and inspections setting the pace. You get the timeline with the quote.

    Main water service and sewer replacements usually do. Small fixes may not. The Winchester building department typically wants a permit for any major buried line replacement, so an inspector can see the depth, bedding and connections before the pipe is covered. We handle the filing.
