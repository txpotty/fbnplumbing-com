    Repiping and Pipe Replacement Choices in Reston, VA

    Whole-Home Repiping in Reston

    Repiping sounds bigger than it is. You can usually stay in the house. We work room by room, restore water most evenings, and keep dust down with plastic sheeting and drop cloths, then patch every opening before we leave. Clean finish.

    PEX Repiping in Reston

    PEX repipes go faster than copper. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, and home-run systems from a manifold let you shut off individual fixtures without killing water to the house. Popular choice.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Replacement in Reston, VA

    Pinhole leaks start small. First a damp spot, then drywall damage. They usually mean the copper wall has thinned from corrosion, so finding one often means others are close. Repipe or patch, we explain.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Reston

    PEX bends around corners and comes in long rolls, so a typical repipe needs fewer fittings than copper would on the same layout. Disconnecting hoses, shutting off outdoor spigots and keeping crawlspace vents closed in winter are easy ways to prevent freeze damage. It often survives freezing where copper would crack. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings where possible, keeping flow strong at each joint. Faster install.

    Full Repipes for Older Homes in Reston

    Homes from the 1950s and 1960s often still have original galvanized pipe. It is near the end. A full repipe brings new PEX or copper to every fixture, improves pressure, clears up discolored water, and removes the leak risk from aging threads. Worth it.

    Whole-Home Re-Piping in Reston

    A whole-home repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture, including the water heater connections. Most single-story homes take two to three days. We run new trunk lines through the attic or crawlspace, branch down to fixtures through small wall openings, with service restored each evening when possible. Then patching.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Reston

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster. Scale and rust do not affect it. Private wells serve many rural Virginia homes, and some well water carries iron, manganese, low pH or sulfur that calls for treatment. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings or PEX-b with crimp rings depending on the job, choosing a layout that fits the house. Fewer joints, fewer leaks.
