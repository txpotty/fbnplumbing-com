    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Reston

    Price depends mostly on fixture count and finish level. A modest three-bedroom house with two baths sits at the low end, while a custom home with four baths, a tankless heater and a recirculation loop costs much more. Our bid comes from the actual plan set. The bid is split by phase so it compares line by line with other plumbers.

    Expect plumbing to touch the schedule three times. Underground takes one to three days. Top-out usually takes three to five days on a single-family house, and trim takes one or two days once cabinets, counters and tile are in place. Inspections add a day each.

    Workmanship is covered under our written warranty, and the length is spelled out in your contract. Manufacturer warranties cover the fixtures themselves. Water heaters and PEX systems often carry 10 years or more, but only when installed with the matching tools and fittings, which is one reason we do not mix brands.

    Scheduling causes most of the trouble. A late pour or a framing change can stack plumbing phases on top of each other. Late plan changes are next, since moving a tub after framing means more drilling and another inspection. We price changes in writing first.

    We can run a dedicated return line during rough-in. Hot water then reaches distant bathrooms in seconds. A dedicated line works better than a crossover valve under a sink, which sends hot water back through the cold line and can leave the cold tap lukewarm for a moment. The pump needs an outlet.

    Soils in Virginia range from sandy ground with a high water table near the coast to dense clay and rocky karst farther inland. Soil movement works on any pipe buried under concrete. We bed drain lines in sand or gravel, sleeve every pipe that passes through concrete, and use flexible PEX for the water service where code allows it. Sleeves are cheap.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drains are PVC or ABS. Cast iron is still used in some multifamily buildings and in walls next to bedrooms where drain noise matters, since it is much quieter than plastic when a toilet flushes upstairs.

    PEX-A is our default for water supply in Virginia homes. It handles heat well and resists the scale and pinhole problems copper can get with aggressive water. Drains are Schedule 40 PVC. Under slabs, we keep joints to a minimum and use long-sweep fittings at every change of direction so a cable or camera can get through later.
