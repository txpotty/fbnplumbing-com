    Pilot Light and Ignition Service in Reston

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. The tech pulls the pilot assembly and checks the thermocouple tip, the orifice and the igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Most trucks carry the common pilot kits.

    Pilot Light Repair in Reston

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Figure on roughly an hour for most pilot repairs.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Reston, VA

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. We pull the burner, brush the ports and blow out the orifice. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. You want a steady blue flame. Rust flakes on the burner send us to check the flue baffle next.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Reston, VA

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. That swap takes about an hour. The new valve gets a leak test at every joint.

    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Reston, VA

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Reston, VA. Most Rheem gas units run a White-Rodgers or Honeywell valve with a blinking status LED. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. Both kits ride on the truck. Genuine parts keep the warranty intact.

    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Reston

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in Reston, VA

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. A lot of the time it is the drain valve, the T&P valve or a dripping supply fitting. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a Reston, VA home. If the bottom seam is wet, the tank has failed. Nothing patches that.

    Venting and Draft Repair in Reston, VA

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. A loose, rusted or sagging section lets exhaust spill back into the house. We fix the vent. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. Spillage gets fixed before we leave.

    Most repairs take one to three hours. Pilot and thermocouple jobs usually wrap up in under an hour.     A thermocouple is a small copper-sheathed rod that sits in the pilot flame and makes a tiny electric current. That current holds a magnet in the gas valve open. If the flame dies, the current stops. When the pilot goes out, the magnet lets go and the valve shuts off gas to both the pilot and the burner, which is the safety that keeps raw gas from filling a closet. A worn one is the top cause of pilot outages.
