    Kitchen Sink Backups: Causes, Prevention and Quick Solutions

    By Dana Whitfield · September 9, 2026 · 5 min read

    Why Kitchen Sinks Back Up

    The kitchen drain takes more abuse than any other drain in the house. Grease and soap go down hot, cool in the pipe, and stick to the walls. In a year or two, that layer can shrink a 1-1/2 inch drain to about the width of a finger. Then one big rinse finds the limit.

    The Usual Causes

    Grease is the main one. Bacon fat and butter turn solid as they cool. Running hot water after pouring grease only pushes it a few feet further, where it sets up in the horizontal run behind the wall. Wipe pans with a paper towel first.

    Garbage disposals grind food, but they do not make it disappear. Eggshells and coffee grounds sink into the trap and pack like sand. Stringy scraps like celery and onion skin wind around the disposal or mat up in the trap. Pasta and rice swell after they go down.

    Water hardness in Virginia varies by region, with harder water in the western limestone valleys and softer water closer to the coast.

    Hard water leaves scale inside older galvanized and cast iron drains, and that rough surface catches grease faster than smooth PVC. Houses built before the 1970s often still have galvanized drain arms in the kitchen wall. Those pipes rust closed from the inside. Chemical cleaners will not keep them open for long.

    Dishwasher and Disposal Connections

    A sink that backs up when the dishwasher drains is a clue. A dishwasher dumps a few gallons quickly, so any partial clog downstream sends that water into the sink basin. So will a brand-new disposal installed with its knockout plug still in the dishwasher inlet. Check that first after a disposal swap.

    Keeping the Line Clear

    Prevention here is cheap. Keep a can for grease and toss it in the trash once it hardens. Use plenty of cold water with the disposal, and let it run about 15 seconds after the motor quiets down. A basket strainer catches the rest.

    Once a month, flush the line. Fill both sink bowls with hot water, then pull the plugs at the same time. The sudden volume scours the trap and the drain arm behind the wall much better than a running faucet ever will. Skip boiling water if you have PVC drains, since repeated boiling water can soften the joints. Hot tap water is plenty.

    Quick Fixes for a Minor Clog

    Start with a cup plunger, not the flange type used on toilets. On a double sink, plug the other drain with a wet rag or a second stopper, or the plunger just pushes air out the other side. Give it ten to fifteen hard strokes. Leave the dishwasher off until it drains.

    Next, pull the trap. Put a bucket under it, loosen the two slip nuts by hand, and clean the U-bend out. Plenty of clogs sit right in that first foot of pipe. A clean trap means the clog is in the wall, and a quarter-inch hand auger is the next tool to reach for.

    Skip chemical drain cleaners. They rarely clear grease clogs and they sit in the trap when they fail. That leaves caustic water for whoever opens the line next, and it can damage older metal drains and the finish on some sinks. Enzyme cleaners are fine for maintenance.

    When to Bring in a Plumber

    Water rising in another fixture changes things. A kitchen sink backup that also gurgles the tub or bubbles up in a floor drain means the clog is in the main line, not the kitchen branch. That takes a cable machine through a cleanout, or a hydro jetter for heavy grease. Do not keep running water.
