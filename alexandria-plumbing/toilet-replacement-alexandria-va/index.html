    Choosing a Toilet Brand in Alexandria, VA

    Replacing Pressure-Assisted Toilets in Alexandria, VA

    Pressure-assisted toilets use a sealed vessel in the tank that stores air under line pressure and pushes water through the bowl hard, which helps in houses with long, low-slope drain runs. They are noticeably louder than gravity toilets. When we replace one, we pick a vessel brand with parts that are easy to get later, and we check that house pressure is in the range the unit needs.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Alexandria, VA

    Toilets made before 1994 often use 3.5 gallons a flush, and some from the 1970s use five or more. A WaterSense model uses 1.28 gallons or less, and the better ones still clear the bowl in one try because the trapway and flush valve are larger than they used to be. Savings show up fast. Check with your water provider about rebates.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in Alexandria

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. Some carriers need new bolts. The new bowl gets matched to the carrier already in the wall whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.

    Commercial Toilet Replacement in Alexandria, VA

    Office and retail restrooms wear out toilets faster than any house, and a replacement there usually means a floor-mount or wall-hung bowl with a flushometer instead of a tank. Work gets scheduled around your business hours. Bowls are matched to the flush valve's gallons per flush, since a mismatch leaves waste behind or wastes water.

    Cracked Bowl or Tank Replacement in Alexandria

    Sometimes only the tank is cracked, and if the model is still in production, a matching tank costs less than a whole new toilet. First we find the model number inside the tank. If the bowl is the cracked part, the whole toilet gets replaced, since the bowl carries the seal to the floor and cannot be patched.

    A standard toilet replacement usually runs $400 to $800 with the new toilet and labor included, depending mostly on the model you choose. Any flange or subfloor repair is extra. Those get their own price before any work, so the base number does not move.

    Most swaps take 1.5 to 2.5 hours. If the flange or subfloor needs work, add up to an hour.

    Plenty. WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, and dual-flush toilets add a half flush of around 0.8 to 1.1 gallons for liquids, which can cut usage further in a busy home. Check the MaP score before buying.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the drain, which you can find by measuring to the closet bolts. Twelve inches is standard. Older homes sometimes have 10 or 14 inch rough-ins, and a toilet made for the wrong one either sticks out or will not fit.

    The toilet's outlet has to line up with the drain, and the rough-in tells you which models will do that without moving pipes. Take the measurement from the finished wall, not the trim. Most are 12 inches. If yours is 10 or 14 inches, only certain models will fit.

    A pro install costs more up front, but it comes with a flange check, the right closet bolts and a warranty if the seal leaks later. A DIY seal that leaks can quietly rot the subfloor. Fixing that costs much more than paying for the install.

    A handle that needs jiggling, a tank that refills on its own, a hiss after the flush or a flush that stops halfway all point to worn tank parts. Each of those parts can be swapped individually. Once a toilet is 25 years old, a full replacement usually beats rebuilding it.
