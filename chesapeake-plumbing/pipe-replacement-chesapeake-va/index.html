    Full Repipes for Older Homes in Chesapeake

    Homes from the 1950s and 1960s often still have original galvanized pipe. That pipe is worn out. A full repipe brings new PEX or copper to every fixture, and fixes pressure and water color at once. Worth it.

    Slab Leak Pipe Replacement in Chesapeake

    Rerouting is often better than digging. Jackhammering floors is a last resort. We run new pipe through the attic or walls to bypass the leaking line under the concrete, which leaves the floors alone and prevents the same line from failing again. Fast too.

    PEX Repiping in Chesapeake

    PEX repipes go faster than copper. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, while manifold setups give you separate shutoffs. Popular choice.

    Whole-Home Re-Piping in Chesapeake

    A whole-home repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture, including the water heater connections. Most single-story homes take two to three days. We run new trunk lines through the attic or crawlspace, branch down to fixtures through small wall openings, with service restored each evening when possible. Then patching.

    Drain Pipe Replacement in Chesapeake, VA

    Drain lines fail too. Old cast iron rusts through. We replace cracked cast iron, failing ABS or old galvanized drains with PVC, checking slope and venting so the new drains flow freely. Camera first.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Chesapeake

    PEX bends around corners and comes in long rolls, so a typical repipe needs fewer fittings than copper would on the same layout. Disconnecting hoses, shutting off outdoor spigots and keeping crawlspace vents closed in winter are simple ways to prevent freeze damage. PEX can stretch a little in a freeze instead of splitting. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings where possible, which give a full-bore connection and hold up well over time. Faster install.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Chesapeake

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster. Scale and rust do not affect it. Private wells serve many rural Virginia homes, and some well water carries iron, manganese, low pH or sulfur that calls for treatment. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings or PEX-b with crimp rings depending on the job, choosing a layout that fits the house. Fewer joints, fewer leaks.
