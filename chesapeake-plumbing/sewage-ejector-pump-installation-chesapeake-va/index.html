    Liberty ejector pumps are a common pick on our jobs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models.     Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Chesapeake

    Zoeller's sewage line runs from light residential to heavy duty, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. Tall lifts need bigger pumps.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Chesapeake

    Residential ejector systems in Chesapeake serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. Each system needs a sealed basin, a pump with a float, a check valve and a vent. Simple parts, exact install. Install mistakes cause more trouble than hardware, so the vent and lid seal get extra attention.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Chesapeake

    Digging the pit means cutting the slab and excavating below it, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Soils in Virginia range from sandy ground with a high water table near the coast to dense clay and rocky karst farther inland. We bed the basin on gravel and backfill around it so it cannot float when groundwater rises, then pour new concrete to seal the floor and lock the basin in place. Floating basins crack floors.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in Chesapeake, VA

    Upgrading an old ejector does not always mean a new basin. With a sound basin, a new pump, fresh check valve, shutoff and alarm give you most of a new system for far less. Cracked basins get replaced.

    Emergency Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Chesapeake

    An ejector failure usually announces itself with the alarm, followed by slow drains and a smell near the pit.     Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Chesapeake, VA

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. Bigger basins let the pump run longer, fewer times a day, which is easier on the motor and switch, so we size up when the pit serves a laundry or several fixtures. Lids must be gas-tight.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Chesapeake

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. Sump pumps push groundwater out to the yard, ejectors push sewage to the sewer, and only the ejector pit must be sealed and vented against gas. They are not interchangeable.
