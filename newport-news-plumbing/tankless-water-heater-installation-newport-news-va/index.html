    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Newport News, VA

    Upfront, a tankless install in Newport News, VA costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. The gap shrinks over time. Less standby loss, a life of up to 20 years versus roughly 10 for a tank, and replaceable parts all count over the years. We price both side by side.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Newport News

    Gas tankless units in Newport News are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. Gas is the catch. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Newport News

    Conversions in Newport News tend to run a full workday. The old tank gets drained and hauled off, the gas line is upsized and tested, a new vent goes out through a wall or roof, and the unit is mounted, plumbed with isolation valves and fired. Hot water is back by evening. Inspection follows within days.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Newport News, VA

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and replacement parts are widely stocked. We install both condensing and non-condensing Rinnai models, and the right one depends on where the vent can go and how much you want to spend up front. Non-condensing units vent in stainless. Condensing models use PVC vent and a condensate drain.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Newport News

    Sizing a home unit comes down to two numbers: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. With a shower at about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen tap a bit under, two showers and a sink together need around 5 or 6 gallons per minute at the needed rise. It shortens the list quickly.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Newport News, VA

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. Venting changes too. Both are on the estimate.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Newport News

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job since the tank's location may not suit the new unit. Mounting near the gas meter on a garage or outside wall shortens the gas line and venting, though it can add distance to the bathrooms. Both distances matter. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.
