    Toilet Rough-In Adjustments in Newport News, VA

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, usually 12 inches, sometimes 10 or 14 in older homes. Take the measurement from the wall to the closet bolts, not from the baseboard trim. If the new toilet's rough-in does not match, it either sits too far out or will not fit. An offset flange can correct an inch or two without moving the drain.

    Water-Saving Toilet Upgrades in Newport News, VA

    Older toilets are one of the largest indoor water users in a house, and replacing a 3.5 gallon model with a 1.28 gallon one cuts toilet water use by more than half. Dual-flush models go lower for liquids. Our recommendations lean on high MaP scores so the savings do not come with double flushing.

    Commercial Toilet Replacement in Newport News, VA

    Office and retail restrooms wear out toilets faster than any house, and a replacement there usually means a floor-mount or wall-hung bowl with a flushometer instead of a tank. Work gets scheduled around your business hours. Bowls are matched to the flush valve's gallons per flush, since a mismatch leaves waste behind or wastes water.

    Replacing Pressure-Assisted Toilets in Newport News, VA

    Pressure-assisted toilets use a sealed vessel in the tank that stores air under line pressure and pushes water through the bowl hard, which helps in houses with long, low-slope drain runs. They are noticeably louder than gravity toilets. Replacement means matching the vessel brand for parts later, and we check that house pressure is in the range the unit needs.

    Toilet Flange Repair During Replacement in Newport News

    Pulling the old toilet is the only chance to see the flange, and a cracked plastic ring or a rusted cast iron flange will ruin a new install within a year. It gets checked on every replacement. For a cracked flange, a stainless repair ring is often enough, while a flange that sits below the finished floor gets a spacer or extender so the seal compresses properly.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Newport News, VA

    Toilets made before 1994 often use 3.5 gallons a flush, and some from the 1970s use five or more. Current WaterSense models are rated at 1.28 gallons per flush or less, and the better ones still clear the bowl in one try because the trapway and flush valve are larger than they used to be. Savings show up fast. Some water providers offer a rebate, so ask before you buy.

    Commercial Flush Valve Upgrades in Newport News

    Manual flush valves on older commercial toilets leak at the handle and stick open, and swapping to a sensor valve during a replacement improves hygiene without changing the rough-in. You can pick battery or hardwired sensors. Work can happen after hours, and we check that the new fixtures meet accessibility requirements.
